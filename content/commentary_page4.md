## Page 4: Where DER is growing and where wholesale value is

For each local distribution company, the horizontal axis is the average wholesale locational premium across its load nodes, defined as the difference between day-ahead LMP and the Ontario zonal price in matched hours (September 2 to October 3, 2026). The vertical axis is net-metered capacity per 1,000 customers from Page 2. The test covers 33 approved distributors. Distributors whose nodes are too few or too varied are excluded from the correlation and are drawn hollow.

The rank correlation is Spearman rho = -0.13 (p = 0.46, n = 33). That is no detectable relationship. The wholesale premium itself is small, averaging -$0.13/MWh with a range from -$6.03 to +$3.30. It tracks marginal losses almost entirely (correlation 0.96 with the loss component). Only 3 of the 33 distributors have a mean congestion value above $1 in absolute terms.

With 33 observations, a correlation of -0.13 is compatible with a true rank correlation from roughly -0.45 to +0.22 (an approximate 95 percent interval). So the result is an absence of evidence and not evidence of absence. Four further limits apply.

- Eleven of the 33 distributors rest on a single node, including both extremes. Hearst (-$6.03, and the highest adoption) and Sioux Lookout (+$3.30, near the lowest adoption) are illustrations and not robust findings.
- Adoption is a 2024 stock that built up over years, set against 2026 prices for about a month.
- Roof stock, income, installer presence and local programs shape adoption and are not in the data.
- Classifying nodes by name pattern moves results. Five distributors shifted by more than $0.50/MWh when I restricted the calculation to load nodes.

The OEB's review was a qualitative assessment against a value stack. It did not claim an empirical footprint, and this page does not supply one. What the page shows is narrower. Under a flat retail-rate credit, a customer has no financial reason to favour a location, and across the distributors tested, adoption does not line up with the one locational energy signal available in public data. That signal is small and mostly reflects losses, so even a customer paid at LMP would face little locational incentive in this window. If the OEB's tariff is implemented, I would expect any locational steering to come from the transmission and distribution capacity components, where constraints exist, and not from the energy price. The data to test that, such as regional planning needs and non-wires solution locations, is not in this dashboard yet.

This page will be rerun on a longer window as nodal data accumulates, and I will report any change in the sign or size of the correlation.
